The idea in one line

Hold more of the Nasdaq-100 when the market is calm, and less when it is stormy.

Big crashes almost always happen while prices are swinging wildly. Calm markets tend to keep rising. So the plan measures how wildly QQQ has been moving and sets how much to hold. You never pick stocks and never read the news.

Check the weather

After the market closes, look at how much QQQ moved each day over the last month, expressed as a yearly swing (volatility).

Set the mix

Target exposure is 20% divided by that swing. A 20% swing means 100% invested; a 40% swing means 50%; a calm 13% swing means 150% (Standard plan).

Trade only on real changes

Buy or sell at the next morning's open, and only when the target moved 10 points or more from what you hold. Otherwise do nothing.

What to expect

It helps most in crashes

In 2000 to 2002 QQQ lost 83% from its peak; the plan lost 55%. In 2008 QQQ fell 42%; the plan fell 29%.

It lags fast rebounds

After a crash, prices can jump while swings are still large, so the plan is under-invested. It trailed QQQ by 15 points in 2009 and in 2020.

It can still lose a lot

The worst fall was 55%. Expect to lose a third or more of your money in a severe crash, and to trail QQQ in many strong years.
