The idea in one line

Own the eight biggest Nasdaq companies, and hold less of them when the market gets stormy.

A handful of giant companies drive most of the Nasdaq-100's gains. The plan owns just the eight largest, sized by how big each one is. It never uses borrowed money. When prices start swinging wildly, it moves part of the money into a Treasury bill fund and waits for calmer weather. You never research stocks or read the news.

Pick the eight

After the last trading day of each quarter, list the Nasdaq-100 members by company value (share price × shares). Keep the top eight.

Size them by value

Each company gets a share in proportion to its value. Bigger companies get more. In between quarters, let the weights drift.

Check the weather

Measure how much QQQ moved each day over the last month, as a yearly swing. Invested share = 20% ÷ that swing, never more than 100%.

Trade only on real changes

Buy or sell at the next morning's open, only when the invested share moved 10 points or more. Everything else goes into the T-bill fund.

What each kind of weather means

Swing is QQQ's daily moves over the last 21 trading days, scaled to a year. Share of days is February 2011 to today.

What to expect

It is a bet on the giants

The edge over QQQ came from the biggest companies beating the rest, mostly from 2016 on. In 2011, 2013, 2020, 2025 and 2026 so far it trailed QQQ. If the giants stop leading, expect to lag.

It does not avoid big losses

The worst fall was about 34%, close to QQQ's 35%. The weather rule trims risk in choppy markets but eight stocks swing more than a 100-stock fund.

Only about 15 years of history

The test starts in 2011. It has never been through a crash like 2000 to 2002, when the biggest tech names fell 80% or more.
